## Criteria
- **[R1-C1]** Unattended registration inside the policy. Setup: a tenant policy that was created and approved ONCE (a single `replace` then `approve` through the store, before the run); a candidate webinar from an authenticated sender whose organizer email or domain is in the policy scope, on a platform and host in the policy, whose title passes the policy's title rules; the candidate is `auto_approved`
  (no person set `approved` for it); the recorder runs in ATTENDANCE mode (`attendance` true). The first scheduler cycle that selects the candidate registers it: the fake form server receives exactly one POST carrying the policy's name and email, the registration row reaches its registered state with the existing phases, and
  the meeting then goes through the existing join path. No input is supplied other than the state files, the policy document, the clock and the stubs: no per-webinar approval file, no flag, no environment variable a person would set for this webinar. The same holds for a candidate whose `status` is `approved` and whose sender is authenticated.
  Negative control in the same test: with the same candidate and no policy document, zero POSTs (R1-C2).
- **[R1-C2]** Nothing is submitted without the one standing authorization. With each of these, the fake server receives ZERO POSTs, the row ends in an `action_required`-class state, and exactly one alert names the reason: no policy document (reason `unconfigured`/`configuration-required`, existing name); a policy that was saved but never approved (`unapproved`); a policy that was approved and then
  replaced (the replace clears the approval, store :81); an approval that was revoked; a policy document of another tenant; a policy whose stored approval revision no longer matches (corrupt document: `unavailable`). The standing authorization is given once; the test proves that ONE approval covers two different webinars in scope, and that neither needs another step.
  A stored `approvedPolicyRevision` is the only authority: no environment variable, config flag or default creates one.
- **[R1-C3]** Outside the scope: never registered. Each of these gives zero POSTs, a stable reason name from the existing vocabulary (`scope-denied`, `denied`, `not-webinar`, `sender-unverified`, `approval-required`, or an existing one that fits), no registration row beyond what the existing workflow writes, and no alert storm (one per webinar):
  an organizer or domain not in the scope; an unauthenticated sender, with the candidate `auto_approved` AND with `approved` (the rule needs `senderAuthenticated === true` in both, `registration-policy.ts:67`); a registration host not in `allowedHosts`; a platform not in the policy; an `http:` URL, a URL with embedded credentials or a fragment (`registrationConfigurationReason`, :74-78);
  a cancelled webinar; a webinar excluded by the policy (exclusion wins, `registration-policy.ts:64`); a candidate that is not classified as a webinar; a source that is unresolved. The AND of all dimensions is kept: a webinar whose title matches the title words but whose organizer is not in scope is NOT registered (see "Needs the Approver", item 3).
- **[R1-C4]** A form that wants a password, CAPTCHA or payment is never submitted and nothing is done to get around it. On the fake server: a form with an `<input type=password>`; a form with a CAPTCHA widget (a reCAPTCHA-style or hCaptcha-style marker and an image challenge); a form with a card-number or payment field; a form that adds one of these after the first page loads. For each: the fake server receives ZERO POSTs,
  the page script does not click through, solve, request an audio challenge, change user agent or proxy, retry with another profile, or call a solving service (the fake server logs every request, and the test asserts the request list is the page load only), and the row ends in an `action_required`-class state whose alert names the reason (new names are [ASSUMPTION]:
  `registration-needs-credentials`, `registration-captcha`, `registration-payment`) with one plain sentence that a person has to do this one. The reason reaches the alert: a test reads the decoded mail and finds the name; the neutral line "Coverage requires review." and the generic "Coverage: needs-registration." are NOT acceptable here (checker finding: `registrationReason` is dropped on the way to the alert today).
  Detection is conservative: when the page script cannot tell, it does not submit.
- **[R1-C5]** Works in attendance mode, and only there as well as elsewhere. `run-pipeline.mjs:254` no longer disables registration for the attendance loop, and R1-C1 passes in `attendance: true`. The same candidate in the non-attendance (watch) mode behaves as it does today (existing assertions pass unchanged). A test pins both modes with one candidate and compares the POST counts (1 and 1, not 1 and 0).
  Registration in attendance mode is bounded: it runs at most once per webinar per candidate configuration (R1-C7), never inside the capture window of a meeting that is already being recorded in the same slot [ASSUMPTION: a registration is a separate short browser step and must not start while a capture child holds the single slot; the builder states how the attendance loop orders the two; see "Needs the Approver", item 5].
- **[R1-C6]** The bot can read the policy where it runs. REQUIREMENT, not mechanism: when the recorder runs in the container, the policy document that the resolver reads is the same document the operator approved, readable at the time of the run, and a missing or unreadable document gives the `unconfigured`-class refusal of R1-C2 (never a crash, never a silent skip). A test starts the pipeline with the policy directory pointed at a temp folder and proves (a) a present, approved document registers (R1-C1),
  (b) the same run with the folder empty refuses with the named reason and one alert, (c) a document changed (replaced) between two cycles is read fresh each cycle (a revoke takes effect on the next cycle, with zero POSTs after it). How the document reaches the container (bucket, mounted file, image) is NOT decided here: it is a container and deployment question for B4 and P1, and a change to `docker/meeting-bot/*` or a cloud resource is a STOP.
  The builder records in the manifest where `registration-policy-host.ts` and `run-pipeline-deps.mjs:56` look for it (`raw/webinars` today) and that this location is not in the state tar (B4), so the live row cannot be PASSed until the deployment unit makes the document readable by the container; the unit's own check does not depend on that.
- **[R1-C7]** No second submission for the same webinar, across restarts. Test: register webinar W (one POST); then (a) run the cycle again with the process alive: zero further POSTs; (b) tear down the process and start a fresh one on the same state directory: zero POSTs; (c) crash the process after the durable "submitting" marker was written and before the response was recorded (the fake server drops the connection after counting the POST),
  then restart: the row is NOT retried automatically with a second POST; it ends in an `action_required`-class state naming the ambiguity (the existing `submitting` phase rule of `goal-t052.md`; this criterion pins that attendance mode inherits it); (d) the same webinar reached through two source rows (a calendar entry and a mail linked to it, `registration-workflow.ts:20-31` identity) yields one POST;
  (e) the policy replaced and re-approved with a different `configurationHash` does NOT cause a second POST for a webinar already registered. The idempotence key is the webinar identity, not the policy.
- **[R1-C8]** The identity comes only from the policy. The name and email in the POST are exactly the values in the approved policy document's form profile (and `config.operator` as the workflow reads it), and nowhere else: not from the mailbox of whoever runs the recorder, not from the process environment, not from a student record, not from the title of the webinar or the sender, not from a default. A test uses a policy name and email, sets a different `USER`, `EMAIL`,
  `GIT_AUTHOR_EMAIL` and a different Gmail-style account in the fake runner, and asserts the POST body has only the policy values. A policy whose operator email does not validate refuses with the existing `profile-required` (workflow :52-59) and zero POSTs. The POST carries only the fields the policy lists (`firstName`, `lastName`, `email`, `organization` if present): an extra form field the policy does not know (a "phone" box, a "marketing opt-in" check box) is left as it is: a required unknown field means the form is not submitted and the reason is `form-required`.
- **[R1-C9]** [SECURITY CLASS: credential handling and data (checker-tagged)] Nothing sensitive leaves in a mail, a log line or the state file. The alerts of R1-C2 to C4 and C7 carry no registration URL with a token or query secret, no operator email address beyond what the existing alert already shows, no password or card value typed or seen, no page text. Test: a registration URL with `?token=SECRET` and a fake form value; the decoded alert text and the log lines
  (captured) contain neither; the row in `operations.json` or the registration state stores the URL only in the form the existing workflow already stores it (a pin: the diff adds no new place that stores it).
- **[R1-C10]** Others stay as before. Existing assertions of `goal-t052.md` C1 to C14, `registration-workflow.test.ts`, the join-policy tests, `skip-reason-vocabulary.test.mjs`, `lifecycle-alerts.test.mjs`, `alert-kinds.test.mjs` pass with no existing assertion edited, removed or skipped (tests may be added). ## Needs the Approver
1. The standing authorization is the policy document that the store already holds (`replace` then `approve`, once). Is that the form he wants to give once, or does he want a different shape (a separate file, a setting)? The contract needs only that a stored approval is the sole authority (R1-C2).
2. What goes into the policy: his name and email for the form, the trusted senders or domains, the allowed hosts and platforms, and the title words. These are his inputs; the checker has not chosen any of them.
3. He said "title words". Under the present rule title words are only an extra condition on top of an organizer or domain in scope, never enough alone (`registration-policy.ts:65-70`, `validateRegistrationPolicy` :49). Making title words alone enough for an unknown sender would loosen the rule and is a critical amendment that needs his own approval. R1 keeps the AND (R1-I1). Is that acceptable?
4. Is an `auto_approved` webinar (the authenticated-sender auto rule, with no person looking) the same as the "within the standing policy" he meant, or should the unattended path require `status: approved` only? The contract pins `auto_approved` and `approved` both, with the authenticated sender required in both.
5. Order of registration and capture in the one-slot attendance loop (R1-C5): may a registration step wait until no capture is running, even when that is a few minutes before the webinar starts? The alternative needs the two-slot work.
6. A registration that is blocked (password, CAPTCHA, payment, unknown required field) reaches him as one alert and a stop. Is one mail per such webinar what he wants, or a daily list?
